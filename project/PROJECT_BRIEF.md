# Project Brief

## Client Commission
Leadership oversees a large grants portfolio but lacks a consistent view of award concentration, recipient dependence, programme overlap, geographic distribution and whether funding patterns align with strategic intent.

> **Decision to support:** Where should grant-management teams investigate concentration, duplication, under-reach or portfolio imbalance before the next funding cycle?

## Primary Dataset
**Government Grants Register 2024–25 — scheme and award data**  
Publisher: Cabinet Office  
Official source: https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/government-grants-statistics-2024-to-2025

## Delivery Lifecycle
**Discovery → Requirements → Governance → Acquisition → Engineering → Data Quality → Analysis → Decision Framework → BI/Product → Assurance/UAT → Executive Recommendation → Handover**

## Cross-Functional Roles
| Role | Expected contribution |
|---|---|
| Business Analyst | Define grants-management decisions, requirements and stakeholder priorities. |
| Data Governance Analyst | Govern scheme/award definitions, recipient identifiers, lineage and quality. |
| Data Engineer | Prepare reproducible ingestion and grant-recipient analytical model. |
| Programme/Data Analyst | Analyse concentration, reach, scheme mix and portfolio patterns. |
| BI Developer | Build grants portfolio and funding-distribution decision support. |
| Evaluation Analyst | Separate funding-output evidence from genuine outcome/impact claims. |
| Risk/Assurance Analyst | Challenge concentration thresholds and recommendation logic. |

## Business Questions
1. What is the current baseline and where is material variation concentrated?
2. Which data-quality, coverage or definition issues could materially alter the decision?
3. Which segments, areas, assets, recipients or corridors require deeper investigation?
4. Which indicators are suitable for prioritisation, and which should not be used that way?
5. What intervention choices are genuinely supported by evidence?
6. What should management monitor after handover?

## Success Criteria
Traceable requirements; governed definitions/lineage/quality; reproducible transformations; defensible award concentration, recipient patterns, programme mix, geographic distribution, funding overlap and portfolio-balance indicators; transparent decision logic; useful BI/product; UAT evidence; executive recommendation; maintainable handover; visible collaborative contribution.

## Panel Challenge
The review panel may challenge assumptions, denominators, definitions, data quality, thresholds, models, spatial interpretation, governance, recommendations and maintainability.
